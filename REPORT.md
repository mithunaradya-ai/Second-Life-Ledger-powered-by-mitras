# REPORT: Second Life Ledger

## Problem
Homes, colleges, restaurants and offices everywhere generate food, oil, paper, plastic, glass, garden, e-waste and textile waste every day. Most of it is binned because nobody can see its value or knows who would buy it.

## Solution
Second Life Ledger gives each campus a value ladder per waste type and routes every batch to the highest-value use its local buyers allow. It also reports the extra value that a missing buyer type would unlock.

## Innovation
1. **Value ladder:** ranks uses by value, not just "recycle or not".
2. **Blocked-value insight:** turns a sustainability tool into a sourcing tool by showing which buyer to recruit next and what it is worth.
3. **Site-as-data:** site type, currency, prices and buyers are configurable, so one codebase serves a college, a home or a restaurant.
4. **Circular credits:** a comparable impact score across countries.

## Users
Campus sustainability officers, hostel and mess managers, student clubs, and local scrap dealers and recyclers.

## Business model
Free for households and small shops (they supply the volume). Revenue comes from two places:
1. **Institution plan:** Rs 999 per site per month for colleges, hostels, apartments, restaurants and offices (dashboard, impact reports, "money left on the table" insights).
2. **Match fee:** 5% on deals completed through the platform, paid by the buyer (recycler, farm, artisan), who gets a steady, sorted supply.

**Why sites will pay:** the app shows how much value a site is losing from missing buyers. If it shows Rs 3,000 a month of unlocked value, a Rs 999 plan pays for itself about three times over.

**Illustrative unit economics (assumptions, not measured):**
- One site recovering Rs 10,000 of waste value per month: Rs 999 plan + Rs 500 match fee = Rs 1,499 per site per month.
- 100 paying sites = about Rs 1.5 lakh per month, about Rs 18 lakh per year.
- Costs are low: a static front end, plus a small backend and database once shared data is added.

**Go-to-market:** start with Coimbatore colleges and hostels, then apartment complexes and restaurants, and use local kabadiwalas and recyclers as the first buyers.

## Impact (illustrative)
Per 100 kg of mixed waste routed to its top available use: value recovered plus CO2e avoided, shown live in the dashboard.

## Scalability
Static front end, configuration-driven. Next: shared backend, photo classification, pickup scheduling, cross-campus surplus exchange.

## Limitations
Sample prices and emission factors; keyword classifier; browser-only storage.

## 60-second pitch
1. Problem: waste has value but is invisible.
2. Log 10 kg of food waste: show the ladder and the value.
3. Show "money left on the table" on the Boston campus (no farm buyer).
4. Switch campus to Nairobi: currency and buyers change, same app.
5. Show the network leaderboard and say: any college can join in a minute.
